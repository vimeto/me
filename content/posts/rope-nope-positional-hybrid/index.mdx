# RoPE, NoPE, and the positional encoding compromise

The RoPE section in my [architecture-convergence post](/blog/architecture-convergence-swiglu-rmsnorm-rope) ended on a confident note: *rotation is a function, so feeding a larger $m$ is well-defined*. I left it there. Rope-to-Nope is the follow-up I didn't write. *Well-defined* isn't the same as *useful*. It turns out half the layers would rather have no positional signal at all.

sinusoidal (2017) → learned absolute (GPT-2) → relative / T5 bias (2019) → [ALiBi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409) (2021) → [RoPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864) (2021) → [NoPE observation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466) (2023) → [YaRN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071) (2023) → [RoPE-to-NoPE hybrid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18795) (2025) → 2026+ (wavelet positions, periodic-extrapolation, …).

Each of those moves fixed a narrow problem with the previous. Sinusoidal couldn't be trained; learned absolute couldn't extrapolate; T5's bucketed relative bias scaled badly; ALiBi had a fixed decay; RoPE gave you a dial. Then NoPE showed up as a quiet shock (a decoder-only transformer with *no positional signal at all* can still learn position from causal masking alone), and YaRN turned RoPE's extrapolation knob into a real long-context recipe. The 2025 Cohere paper is the one that asks the question nobody quite asked out loud: *what if we mixed them*.

## Four flavours, one axis

Before the hybrid claim, it's worth sitting with what each scheme does to a single query-key pair, assuming $q = k$ before any positional handling (so the pre-position similarity is 1). That isolates the positional signature.

This is a direct generalisation of the `archRopeSimilarity` plot from the earlier post, with the other three schemes overlaid. NoPE being a flat line at 1 is the important visual. It's not that NoPE ignores position; it's that from a *similarity* perspective there is no positional term to plot. Anything NoPE learns about position has to come from the causal mask plus the forward pass itself. ALiBi commits to monotone decay by construction. (ALiBi is technically an additive bias on pre-softmax logits, not a transformation of $q \cdot k$; I'm plotting it on the same axis as the others for visual parity, which is a small abuse worth flagging.) RoPE's ring is what you get when you add up $d/2$ cosines at geometrically-spaced frequencies (low frequencies give long-range coherence, high frequencies drive the short-range wiggle). QK-Norm squashes the range without changing the shape.

<Callout tone="insight" title="Why NoPE works at all in decoder-only models">
  A decoder's causal mask already encodes position indirectly: the token
  at index $m$ can attend to exactly $m$ prior tokens, so the *cardinality*
  of the attention set leaks position into the forward pass. The NoPE
  paper (2023) showed that this leakage is enough for the model to learn
  position implicitly. Not great in-range; surprisingly graceful out of range.
</Callout>

## The extrapolation cliff

The pitch for RoPE has always been that it extrapolates. You rotate by $m \cdot \theta_i$ for any $m$, trained-distribution or not. *Well-defined* is the word I used in the earlier post. But well-defined doesn't mean calibrated: the low-frequency subspaces, at $\theta_i = b^{-2i/d}$ with $b = 10000$ and $d = 128$, have wavelengths around $2\pi \cdot 10000 \approx 63000$ tokens. If you train at $L = 8192$, those subspaces never complete a full rotation during training. At inference past $L_\text{train}$, the model sees phases it has never seen. The output falls off a cliff.

The curves are hand-tuned softplus cliffs, not fitted perplexities — trust the shape, ignore the magnitudes. Pure RoPE has a sharp knee. Pure NoPE is shifted up uniformly (worse in the training range, no cliff at all). YaRN stretches the RoPE curve out by rescaling the low-frequency thetas, at a small in-range cost. The hybrid gets closest to the floor across the whole range.

<Callout tone="warn" title="The knee is a phase problem, not a length problem">
  RoPE doesn't break because positions get "too large". It breaks
  because the combination of $m$ and $\theta_i$ lands at phases the model
  never saw. Raise the base $b$ and you shift the knee out (fewer
  subspaces wrap during training). Base-scaling and YaRN are both
  addressing the same geometry, with different dials.
</Callout>

## Why mix layers

The core claim in [RoPE-to-NoPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18795) (2025): you don't want every layer doing the same thing. Their Command-R-style recipe interleaves **1 NoPE full-attention layer for every 3 RoPE sliding-window layers** (so NoPE at 25% of layers, RoPE at 75%, and the NoPE layer sits at the *end* of each group of four). RoPE layers get a 4096-token sliding window; NoPE layers get full attention. The model is pretrained on 5T tokens at 8k context, then extended to 32k (10k steps) and 128k (5k steps).

The headline results are in two places. On RULER retrieval at 256k context, their hybrid scores 74.8 vs 57.1 for a pure-RoPE baseline (that's a 30% relative improvement where it matters). On RULER QA at 256k, 42.5 vs 30.0. Efficiency-wise the win is almost better: **~50% faster training throughput at 64k context, nearly 2× faster at 128k**, and **44% lower inference latency at 132k input, ~70% at 990k**. Because sliding-window RoPE layers don't need full KV cache, the hybrid gets up to 75% KV-cache reduction.

So the "compromise" in the title is a bit of a rhetorical feint. It's not a compromise; it's a strict improvement on all three axes.

Two things worth playing with. First, push the context length past 128k and the recall-optimal $r$ drifts down from near-1 (pure RoPE is fine at short context) toward the 0.7–0.8 band. Second, the Cohere paper's placement insight (the NoPE layer sits *after* the RoPE layers in each group, not before) doesn't come out of the toy model here, but the qualitative claim does: mixing beats either extreme.

## The frequency spectrum view

One more angle worth seeing. RoPE's $\theta_i$ spectrum is a log-linear line in $i$. The "usable" subspaces (the ones that complete at least one full rotation inside the training context) form a band; everything to the right is in phase-space the model hasn't seen.

This is the geometric reason YaRN and NTK-aware scaling work. Raising $b$ from 10000 to 500000 doesn't change the attention story at short range (high-frequency subspaces are unaffected); it just slides the low-frequency tail into *larger* wavelengths, so those subspaces go from "never rotated once" at 8k context to "rotated a handful of times" at 128k. The hybrid sidesteps this argument entirely in the NoPE layers (no rotation, no phases to worry about) and keeps it in the RoPE layers (short-range structure, cheap sliding window).

What I like about this whole arc is that it reads like a small lesson about architecture design: once you stop asking "which positional encoding is best" and start asking "which positional encoding is best *at each layer*", the answer falls out. My earlier post framed RoPE as the terminal winner on the positional axis. That's wrong. RoPE plus the option to opt out is the winner. The cleanest version of any architectural choice often ends up being a mixture of two simpler ones, and this is one more example.
